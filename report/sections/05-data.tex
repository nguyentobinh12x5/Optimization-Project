\section{Data}\label{sec:data}

Constituents of the VN100 index were obtained through the \texttt{vnstock} library
\citep{vnstock}, and $N = 98$ tickers survive cleaning. The price panel spans
\textbf{2022-07-26 to 2026-07-24} ($997 \times 98$), and the derived simple-return panel spans
\textbf{2022-07-27 to 2026-07-24} ($965 \times 98$). All results in this report use that
return panel.

Two defects in the vendor feed were found and corrected, and both matter enough to name.
First, \textbf{phantom weekend sessions}: the source occasionally repeated the previous
session's close under a Saturday or Sunday date. Left in place, these inject spurious
zero-return days that pull $\muhat$ toward zero and deflate every entry of $\Sig$ --- they
corrupt precisely the two inputs the model consumes. They are removed by dropping rows falling
on a weekend. Second, \textbf{inconsistent intraday timestamps across sources}: the primary
and fallback endpoints return different times of day for the same trading session, so merging
several tickers silently doubles the row count, producing a panel riddled with missing values
that no ticker actually has. Normalizing every timestamp to its calendar date before the merge
resolves it.

A weekday filter cannot catch Vietnamese public holidays falling on a weekday --- Tet, 30
April, 1 May, 2 September --- on which the same repeated-price artifact can appear. Rather
than maintaining a hard-coded holiday calendar, which would need updating every year and would
silently rot, the loader applies a statistical rule: any session on which at least $90\%$ of
tickers post \emph{exactly} zero return is treated as a repeated-price artifact and dropped.
On the present dataset this filter removes no additional rows. It is a guard against future
data rather than a fix for a problem in the current panel, and it is reported that way here
because claiming otherwise would overstate the cleaning that actually took place.

One structural limitation is worth stating before any result is presented. The \emph{current}
VN100 membership is applied backward across the full four years, so companies that left the
index or were delisted during the period are absent from the universe. The sample is therefore
biased toward survivors, which tends to flatter any strategy evaluated on it, including the
benchmark. This is revisited in \cref{sec:conclusion}.
