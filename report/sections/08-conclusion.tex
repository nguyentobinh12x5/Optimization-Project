\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Computational efficiency and solution quality}

The hand-written solver required between \textbf{222 and 2113} iterations across the
verification grid, each dominated by one matrix--vector product $\Sig w$ at $O(N^2)$ plus the
bisection of \cref{prop:jointprox} at $O(N \log(1/\epsilon))$; no factorization occurs inside the
loop. The contrast with the interior-point method is the expected one in kind: CLARABEL converges
in a few dozen iterations, but each forms and factorizes a KKT system at roughly $O(N^3)$. It
would be convenient to conclude that the first-order method therefore wins, and the data does not
support that --- at $N = 98$ a dense factorization is trivial and neither method is near a
wall-clock bottleneck. The first-order method's advantage here is transparency and its derivation
from the problem structure; its asymptotic advantage would become real only at $N$ in the
thousands. On quality, the solver produces exact zeros rather than thresholded small values,
satisfies the budget constraint to $10^{-12}$, and matches an independent interior-point solver
to a maximum relative objective gap of $7.75 \times 10^{-6}$ with an exactly reproduced active
set on every parameter set tested (\cref{tab:cvxpy}).

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Survivorship bias:} current VN100 membership applied backward over four years,
        so delisted and removed tickers are absent.
  \item \textbf{$\muhat$ remains a poor estimator:} high variance over a short window; the robust
        term blunts the consequences but no term removes them.
  \item \textbf{The $\kappa$ finding is empirical:} an observation on one covariance structure,
        not a general result.
  \item \textbf{The out-of-sample window is short:} roughly 24 months spanning a single market
        regime, so \cref{sec:backtest} cannot be extrapolated to other cycles.
  \item \textbf{Validation overfitting:} selecting $(\kappa,\gamma)$ on a six-month window is a
        mild form of fitting to the validation set --- inherent to short-window walk-forward
        rather than a defect of this implementation, and the direct cause of the turnover driving
        the cost disadvantage.
  \item \textbf{A single historical path:} no bootstrap, so no confidence interval accompanies
        the reported Sharpe ratio or drawdown.
  \item \textbf{Costs are modelled crudely:} a flat $0.20\%$ of turnover, with no market-impact
        or slippage term.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conclusion}

The problem was formulated as a convex, second-order-cone representable program in which the
robust term is derived as an exact worst-case objective over an ellipsoidal uncertainty set
rather than assumed, and the $\ell_1$ penalty serves both as a convex surrogate for the number of
holdings and as a cap on gross leverage. A proximal-subgradient method was implemented in pure
NumPy, resting on an exact joint proximal operator for the $\ell_1$ penalty and the budget
constraint taken together (\cref{prop:jointprox}); it reproduces an independent interior-point
solver's optimal value to $7.75 \times 10^{-6}$ relative gap and its active set exactly, on every
parameter set tested. Out of sample the resulting long-only strategy beat an equal-weight
benchmark on a risk-adjusted basis over 494 trading days --- by a real but modest margin, at
materially higher volatility, deeper drawdown, and roughly seven times the cost.

The methodological lesson is that of \cref{sec:proxbug}: the original solver composed two
individually correct operations into an incorrect one, returning portfolios that were feasible,
plausible, and wrong by several percent in objective and by a factor of five in positions held.
Nothing in the output revealed it --- it was found only by solving the problem again with an
unrelated algorithm and comparing. For convex problems, where a unique optimum makes that
comparison decisive, the independent check is worth more than any inspection of the primary
implementation.\label{sec:endofbody}
