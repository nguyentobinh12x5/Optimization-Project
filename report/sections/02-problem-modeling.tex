\section{Problem Modeling}\label{sec:modeling}

\subsection{Decision Variable and Constants}

The \textbf{decision variable} is the weight vector $w \in \R^{N}$ with $N = 98$, where $w_i$
is the fraction of total capital allocated to stock $i$; a negative $w_i$ denotes a short
position.

The \textbf{constants} are statistical inputs estimated from historical data before optimization
and held fixed while it runs: $\muhat \in \R^{N}$, the sample mean of daily returns;
$\Sig \in \mathbb{S}^{N}_{+}$, their sample covariance; and $\Ssqrt$, the symmetric
positive-semidefinite square root of $\Sig$, so $\Ssqrt\Ssqrt = \Sig$. The scalars
$\kappa, \gamma, \lambda \ge 0$ are \textbf{hyperparameters, not decision variables}: the
optimizer treats them as fixed, chosen by the modeller when exploring the solution family
(\cref{sec:insample}) or by validation on held-out data (\cref{sec:backtest}).

All quantities are in \textbf{daily} return units; annualized figures appear only in the
performance table of \cref{sec:backtest}.

\subsection{Objective Function}

The general objective combining all four terms of \cref{sec:identification} follows the robust
and sparse mean--variance formulation of \citet{chen2024}, adopted here as the single unifying
model rather than derived from scratch: their model is the origin of the $\kappa\norm{\Ssqrt w}_2$
robustness term and the $\lambda\norm{w}_1$ sparsity term alongside the classical Markowitz return
and variance terms. \Cref{sec:backtest} makes precise how each of the six variants tested there is
obtained from this one objective by fixing or dropping terms --- no variant introduces anything
not already present here. The optimization problem is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
\min_{w \in \R^{N}} \quad
  \underbrace{-\muhat^\top w}_{\text{expected return}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\kappa \norm{\Ssqrt w}_2}_{\text{robustness}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\gamma\, w^\top \Sig w}_{\text{variance}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\lambda \norm{w}_1}_{\text{sparsity}}
\qquad \text{s.t.} \quad \one^\top w = 1 .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Constraints}

The single explicit constraint is the \textbf{budget constraint} $\one^\top w = 1$: fully
invested. \textbf{There is no constraint $w \ge 0$} --- short selling is permitted, a deliberate
choice, not an omission, to bear in mind in \cref{sec:insample}. \Cref{sec:backtest} does add
$w \ge 0$ for the deployed strategy and explains why the out-of-sample study is long-only while
the in-sample study is not.

\subsection{Type of Optimization Problem}

Problem \cref{eq:main} is \textbf{convex, nondifferentiable, and second-order-cone
representable}. Convexity is argued in \cref{sec:correctness}; conic representability follows
from the epigraph reformulation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:socp}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{w,\,t,\,s,\,u} \quad & -\muhat^\top w + \kappa t + \gamma s + \lambda \one^\top u \\
\text{s.t.} \quad
  & \norm{\Ssqrt w}_2 \le t, \quad
    \norm{\Ssqrt w}_2^2 \le s, \quad
    -u_i \le w_i \le u_i\ (i=1,\dots,N), \quad
    \one^\top w = 1 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first constraint is a second-order cone on $(\Ssqrt w, t)$; the second is a rotated
second-order cone, using $w^\top \Sig w = \norm{\Ssqrt w}_2^2$; the third is the standard linear
splitting of the $\ell_1$ norm, tight at the optimum since $\lambda \ge 0$; the fourth is affine.
Every constraint is affine or conic and the objective linear in $(w,t,s,u)$, so \cref{eq:main} is
a second-order cone program --- exactly what CLARABEL, the independent reference against which
\cref{sec:verification} checks this report's own solver, is built for.

Nondifferentiability is why \cref{eq:main} cannot be solved by plain gradient descent: two terms
are nonsmooth, $\kappa\norm{\Ssqrt w}_2$ at $\Ssqrt w = 0$ and $\lambda\norm{w}_1$ wherever some
$w_i = 0$ --- exactly where sparse solutions live. This report responds two ways: \cref{eq:socp}
\emph{sidesteps} the nonsmoothness, moving every nonsmooth term into a conic constraint for an
interior-point solve; \cref{sec:algorithm} instead \emph{confronts} it, splitting the objective
into a subgradient part and a part with a closed-form proximal operator --- the method actually
implemented, with the conic route kept as the verifier.

\subsection{Modeling Assumptions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Daily returns are drawn from a distribution whose first two moments are stable across the
        estimation window, so a sample mean and covariance are meaningful forward-looking inputs.
  \item $\muhat$ and $\Sig$ are the only inputs; the model contains no separate forecasting layer.
  \item Capital is perfectly divisible, and there are no position, sector, or liquidity limits
        beyond the budget constraint.
  \item Transaction costs are \emph{absent} from \cref{eq:main}, reintroduced only in the
        backtest at $0.20\%$ of turnover per rebalance as a post-hoc deduction rather than a term
        the optimizer sees --- the trade-offs of that choice are discussed once the empirical
        cost impact is on the table (\cref{sec:results7}).
  \item Short positions are assumed executable without borrowing cost, and weights re-set at each
        evaluation point --- except in \cref{sec:backtest}, where drift between rebalances and
        the cost of correcting it are modelled explicitly.
\end{enumerate}
