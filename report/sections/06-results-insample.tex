\section{In-Sample Results}\label{sec:insample}

\subsection{Estimating the inputs}

$\muhat$ is the column-wise sample mean of the return panel and $\Sig$ its sample covariance
($\text{ddof} = 1$), symmetrized. $\Ssqrt$ is built by \textbf{eigendecomposition rather than
Cholesky}: at $N=98$, $T=965$, the sample covariance is positive \emph{semi}definite at best, with
a few marginally negative eigenvalues in floating point on which Cholesky --- requiring strict
positive definiteness --- fails. The implementation clips negative eigenvalues to zero and
reconstructs $\Sig$ to relative Frobenius error $1.831\times10^{-15}$; the same near-boundary
tolerance is relaxed via \texttt{cp.psd\_wrap} when $\Sig$ is passed to CVXPY, without altering the
data the solver sees.

\subsection{Solving the problem, and the effect of $\lambda$}

Problem \cref{eq:main} is solved with the proximal-subgradient method of \cref{sec:algorithm},
every solve cross-checked against CVXPY/CLARABEL (\cref{sec:verification}). \Cref{tab:demo}
reports two representative solves; $\one^\top w = 1$ holds to $10^{-12}$ and the objectives agree
to six significant figures. ``Active'' means $|w_i| > 10^{-4}$ --- needed only for the
interior-point reference, which drives inactive coordinates to $10^{-9}$--$10^{-6}$ rather than
zero; the proximal step returns exact zeros (\cref{prop:exactzero}) that match CLARABEL's active
set exactly (\cref{tab:verification}), so the counts below are exact, not threshold-dependent.

Active positions fall monotonically from $89$ to $17$ and then \textbf{plateau} across the top
decade and a half of the grid (\cref{fig:sparsity}) --- not a numerical floor but a direct
consequence of \cref{eq:leverage}: at $\lambda \approx 4\times10^{-3}$ the last short position
closes and $\norm{w}_1$ reaches exactly $1$, so $\lambda\norm{w}_1 \equiv \lambda$ becomes a
constant that stops discriminating between feasible points. This is the same vacuity
\cref{sec:backtest} invokes to drop $\lambda$ from A/B's search grids, reached here
\emph{endogenously} rather than imposed by $w\ge0$: sparsity and the elimination of short selling
are one effect of $\lambda$ on this universe, not two.

\subsection{The effect of \texorpdfstring{$\gamma$}{gamma}}\label{sec:gammasensitivity}

\Cref{fig:gammasens} sweeps the risk-aversion coefficient $\gamma$ over $[0.1,100]$ with
$\kappa=\lambda=0$ fixed, isolating its effect from the constraints A/B add later. Both curves
fall monotonically and smoothly --- unlike the $\lambda$ sweep, $\gamma$ enters through a strictly
convex quadratic with no active set to reorganize. At $\gamma=0.1$ the optimizer takes a
$137\%$-volatility, $37\%$-return position, an unconstrained-leverage bet only sane against a
fixed in-sample $\muhat$ and the same mechanism \cref{sec:ef} later shows causes catastrophic
out-of-sample losses; at $\gamma=100$ both curves flatten near the minimum-variance corner. The
long-only variants of \cref{sec:backtest} search $\gamma\in\{1,5,10\}$, the steep part of this
curve --- consistent with $\gamma$'s selected value swinging month to month rather than settling
(\cref{sec:results7}).

\subsection{The effect of the robust term}

Raising $\kappa$ from $0$ to $1$ \textbf{increases} both active positions and concentration
(Herfindahl--Hirschman index; \cref{app:robust}) --- the opposite of the folk intuition that
robust means diversified. The mechanism is degree-one homogeneity (\cref{eq:robust-derivation}):
$\kappa\norm{\Ssqrt w}_2$'s subgradient does not shrink with $\norm{w}$ the way the variance
gradient $2\gamma\Sig w$ does, so it pulls with constant, scale-independent magnitude in a fixed
direction --- efficiently served, given a covariance dominated by a single market factor, by
concentrating in a low-variance combination of a few names rather than spreading across many
correlated ones. An empirical observation on this dataset with a plausible mechanism, not a
theorem for every $\Sig$; portfolio weights at small vs.\ large $\lambda$ are compared in
\cref{app:weights}.
